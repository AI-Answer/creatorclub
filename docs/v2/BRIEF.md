# CreatorClub v2: prototype brief (shared by prototypes A, B and C)

## Who and what
CreatorClub is a local desktop app for YouTube creators. The pipeline: **find what is going viral → turn it into an idea → plan and structure the video → record → edit → publish**. The user (Dani, founder) rejected the current UI as overloaded and confusing, and asked for a rethink from a UX perspective: simplify to make it useful, **not empty**. He likes the current visual styling (tokens in `docs/prototype/styles.css`), so keep that look: warm neutral surfaces, Inter, black primary buttons, blue accent, red record.
Dani's words: "so many buttons, so many things that are not clear, things that are incoherent"; "recording should be super easy and seamless"; "editing should be seamless"; "I can have my chat stuck on the right part, and it is my companion across the whole end-to-end process"; the timeline is "super bad"; he wants "a quick preview", "motion graphics and different styles", and publishing "done with Postiz".

## Must-haves in every prototype (one HTML file, hash routes)
Routes: `#/discover` (what is going viral, turn one into an idea, ideas list), `#/plan`, `#/record`, `#/edit`, `#/publish`, `#/settings`. Use Samin's video "Hidden Features of Your AI Tool" with its four scenes (Why this matters 1:00 · Level 1: one-click features 2:15 · Level 2: a workflow that changes how you work 3:00 · Level 3: set it up once, reuse it 2:00), plus real titles, hooks and talking points from `docs/prototype/data.js`.

1. **One docked companion chat, right side, on every step** (the "Ask Claude" companion). It shows what it is looking at ("Looking at: Plan · Scene 2"), has suggestions that change per step, answers with canned but realistic replies, and its replies can **act** on the screen with a visible result and Undo (for example: adds a hook, drafts scenes, tightens a script, removes pauses, adds a lower third at 0:12, applies a caption style, writes the description). Collapsible, resizable, one conversation per video that persists across steps.
2. **Simplicity budget.** At first paint no screen shows more than about 25 interactive controls (count them with a script and report the numbers per screen). One navigation system. One obvious primary action per screen. Anything advanced sits behind one "More" or a hover. Not empty: each screen shows the information that helps the next decision.
3. **Discover**: a ranked list of videos working right now for the creator's topics, each with the multiplier ("43× Nate Herk's usual"), a small thumbnail only where it carries information, "Break down" that opens an inline breakdown (why it worked) and concepts to turn into ideas, then "Start planning".
4. **Plan**: the research board (references, notes) and the package (title, thumbnail text, hook) and the scenes with talking points, in a layout that does not repeat the same information. Scenes are the spine of the rest of the video.
5. **Record**: seamless. One big Record action for the current scene; sources summarized as one line ("Camera · Microphone · Screen: ready") with one place to fix them; a pre-flight warning only when something is wrong; the scene's talking points as a teleprompter; countdown; a floating recording bar ("island") concept shown; after stopping, the take preview with three choices (Delete take · Record another take · Continue); Continue **puts the take on the timeline automatically** and moves to the next scene; per-scene take thumbnails; at the end, one "Continue to edit".
6. **Edit**: a big preview; a **transcript** beside it (delete words to cut, the spoken word highlights); and a **great timeline** that you can demo in the prototype with real JavaScript: filmstrip thumbnails and waveform, scene markers, linked screen/camera/audio per take (move and trim together, with an option to unlink), magnetic snapping with a visible snap line, trim handles, split at playhead (S), ripple delete, drag with a ghost, multi-select and marquee, zoom that anchors at the playhead, fit, scrubbing and hover-scrub, ruler with ticks, user markers, tracks that appear only when used. **Quick preview**: hovering or arrow-keying through styles, caption styles and auto-edits shows the result in the stage immediately, with a before/after toggle and "Previewing at lower quality" notice. **Auto-edit** in one place: remove pauses (aggressiveness and padding), filler words, auto zoom on the cursor, captions, style. **Motion graphics** as draggable timeline blocks the user or the companion can add: title card, lower third, callout/arrow, animated captions, chapter label, with a few styles each, previewed live with CSS animation. Edit versions with "Restore this version".
7. **Publish**: Postiz is the main route (paste your Postiz API key once; then pick the connected YouTube channel), with "Upload straight to YouTube" (Sign in with Google) as the second option. Fields: title, description, tags, thumbnail, visibility, made for kids (required), category (direct route only), publish now or schedule. Export is part of this step (one line: "Exporting 1080p… 2 of 4 scenes"), then upload progress and the result with a link. Include a failed state with one plain sentence and Retry.
8. **Settings**: simple, in three groups only (Connected AI, Channel, Publishing & look). Plain words, no jargon.
9. **Beginner**: the first-launch flow is not required, but the Discover empty state for a brand-new channel (no channel, no followed channels) must be shown as one state with one next action.

## Differences between variants (your assignment says which one you build)
- **A: Guided path.** One clear task per screen, a thin step rail, big primary action, minimal chrome, the companion docked right at a fixed width. Best for beginners.
- **B: Studio.** A three-pane workspace (scenes rail left, stage centre, companion right) with a context dock below (timeline in Edit, sources in Record, scene scripts in Plan). One step switcher at the top. Denser but organized with progressive disclosure. Best for power users.
- **C: Conversation-first.** The companion is wide and primary. The centre shows the artifact the conversation is producing (board, package, script, takes, edit, publish form) and manual controls appear on hover or under "Edit by hand". Fewest buttons.
