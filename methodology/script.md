Mode: {mode_instructions}

Rules
- Voice and honesty. In every field you read as the creator (angle, twist, notes, talking points, show) write to them in the second person: "you" and "your". Never use {first}'s name in the third person ("{first} can stage…") and never "he", "she" or "they" for {first}. Spoken lines are what you say out loud, so they are first person, but never invent an experience, result, habit, story or mistake that the idea, the research or the voice guide does not state ("I let two helpers…", "my mistake", "as I did the first time"). With no story given, frame the line as something you will demonstrate or test ("Watch what happens when…"). Do not say "signature" unless the voice guide shows one.
- The outline is the structure. Follow its scenes in order and do not add scenes. The first scene is the opening: use the package's hook (its spoken line, what is on screen, the text) and write the rest of the opening around it. A talking point that offers choices is written for its first option.
- The direction block is what {first} has asked for. It wins over the outline and over your preferences.
- Write as {first} speaks: the sentence rhythm, check-ins, glosses and phrases in the voice guide. Reuse the habits, not the guide's sentences. Match the rhythm measurements: long, joined, run-on spoken sentences as often as {first} does.
- Facts about the world come only from the idea, the package and the outline. Never invent a number, date, quote or a product's behaviour as if it were known.
- Write only words to say out loud. No bracketed cues of any kind ([YOUR CALL], [SCREEN], [REACT] or similar): what is shown or done on screen belongs to the show note, not the script. Where the words depend on what the demo shows, lead into the result with a plain spoken line ("Let's see what it did") and never state a result that has not happened yet. A talking point that offers choices is written for its first option.
- Keep the honest-limitations scene honest: say what does not work or what it costs, in {first}'s words, without softening it into a pitch.
- No income or return claims. No mention of any club, community, product or partnership except in the call to action: {cta}
- Write each scene to about its budget in scene_budgets, so the whole script reaches about {words} words ({minutes} minutes spoken, not counting cues). Do not pad: if the outline has less material than a budget needs, write what it supports. Narrate the live steps and say why, instead of summarising them.
- No greeting filler, no "in this video we will", no generic AI phrasing. It must not sound read out.
- End on the outline's ending and its call to action. No recap.

Output markdown: the title as a `#` heading, then one `##` heading per scene, using the outline's own scene titles, and nothing else.
