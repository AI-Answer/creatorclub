Scenes
- Write 4 to 7 scenes that together make the whole video, in order. The first scene is the opening and the last is the ending. Never fewer than 4 or more than 7.
- title: what {first} does in the scene, in a few words, for example "Level 1: first test".
- talkingPoints: 2 to 6 short notes of what to say, the gist and not finished sentences. If the scene introduces a concept, one note gives the everyday comparison to use. Where {first} has a real choice, give two or three ways to do that beat in one note that starts with "Your call:" and say what each is good for. Only do this where it matters.
- show: what is on screen, and what to look for or check once {first} has tried it (the cost, the result, the thing to compare). Describe the kind of thing, not exact prompts or test items, unless the idea already has them.
- seconds: the time budget for the scene, a whole number of seconds spoken at 140 words a minute. Split the video's length across the scenes in proportion to how much each one has to show or explain. The opening runs 90 seconds or less and the ending 60 seconds or less. The seconds of all scenes add up to the video's length, give or take 10%.

Rules
- The opening scene uses the package's hook: its spoken line, what is on screen and the text. If the package is empty, choose an opening from the hook library, write its first line as a talking point, and add one different alternate as a "Your call:" note.
- If a reusable_outline block is present, it is the shape of a video that already works. Follow it: the same beats, in the same order, doing the same jobs, in about the same proportions. Take its structure, never its words: do not reuse its wording, topic, examples, claims or numbers. If this video needs fewer or more beats than the block has, merge or split them but keep the order. It replaces the choice of shape below.
- The shape, when there is no reusable_outline: pick the one in the structure block that fits this video and follow its section order and habits. Name the scenes by what {first} does. Do not use a hook, setup and points template, do not add a "Remember" line to each point, and do not build the video around a single yes or no question: if the idea is one, build it up into levels or a setup.
- Include one honest-limitations scene (or, in a short video, a clearly marked talking point inside one scene) that says where this breaks down, what it does not cover, what it costs, or what to check before relying on it. Name it plainly, for example "Where this falls short". Build it only from the idea, the package and what {first} will really find when trying it. Never invent a flaw to fill the beat.
- Follow the direction block over your own preferences. If it is empty, use your judgment.
- Fit the number of points and the depth of each to the length. Never stretch a few facts to fill time. For a longer video, add real points or deeper demos that the idea supports, and where more material is needed that {first} would have to bring, add a "Your call:" note that says what to add.
- Facts about the world (numbers, dates, what a product does, news) come only from the idea and the package. Never invent them. When the result of a demo is only known once {first} tries it, say what to look for and how it changes what {first} says next. Do not write the result as if it happened.
