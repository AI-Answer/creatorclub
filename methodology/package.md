Fields
- demand, gap, show: each a level (high, mid or low) and a note of one short sentence. Demand is evidence that people want this (the research above, views beating the channel's usual, viewers asking). Gap is how open the topic is: high means little competition. Show is how easily {first} can demonstrate it with their own hands. When the input gives no evidence for a rating, rate it mid and write "not checked" in the note.
- angle: what this video does, one sentence.
- twist: what makes it {first}'s: what {first} would show or do differently from what is already out there, one sentence.
- titles: exactly three working titles, most proven first.
  - Tier 1: follows the pattern of {first}'s own titles most closely (see recent_titles and the structure block). The safe bet.
  - Tier 2: the same pattern remixed with an angle from the research. A calculated risk.
  - Tier 3: a pattern break or contrarian angle {first} has not tried. Still honest.
  - Each title is 7 words or fewer and 60 characters or fewer, counting every space. It names one specific thing and is a bold claim or a fix. Never a question, never a yes or no framing.
  - Put the strongest word first. Specific beats vague. A curiosity gap needs a credibility signal beside it.
  - The video must deliver exactly what the title says. No clickbait that misstates a fact, no income or return claims.
  - Leave out filler such as "Ultimate Guide", "Everything You Need to Know" and "Complete Tutorial". No brackets, no emoji, no starting with the channel name.
- thumbnailText: 2 to 5 words for the thumbnail image, in capitals. It works with the title and does not repeat it: the title and the thumbnail text together leave an open question. Concrete beats abstract ("$0 A MONTH", "ONE COMMAND"). Use only things {first} can really show.
- hook: the opening as three parts that point at the same idea.
  - Choose one pattern from the hook library that fits what is true about this video (the category selector tells you where to start), and fill it with real specifics from the idea and research. Do not name the pattern.
  - visual: what is on screen in the first 1 to 3 seconds, using only things {first} can really show.
  - spoken: what {first} says in the first 5 to 15 seconds. One to three short sentences, under 25 words in all, at a fifth-grade reading level, in {first}'s voice. It opens a question the video answers.
  - text: the words on screen. They complement the spoken line and do not repeat it.
  - Check it against the four commandments before you answer. Alignment: visual, spoken and text point at the same idea. Speed to value: no delay and no fluff before it lands. Clarity: a viewer knows in one sentence what the video is about. Curiosity: it opens a question in the viewer's mind. If one fails, fix the hook.

- No income or return claims. No mention of any club, community, product or partnership.
