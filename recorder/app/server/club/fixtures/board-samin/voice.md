## 1. Summary
A hands-on, upbeat tutor for beginners and small-business owners who want to use AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT/Codex, Higgsfield, Buzz, Hermes) to make content, automate work or make money. He narrates a screen, one click at a time, and explains each concept with an everyday comparison before building. He promises that a non-technical viewer can follow ("all we're doing is simply speaking to Claude" in 6 of 9). He speaks from his own use, says what things cost him, and recommends his community at the end. Casual and warm, not shouty.

## 12. Write like this (10 items)
1. Open with the result and a big claim in one or two sentences, then say how many levels or steps the video has.
2. Promise that a beginner can follow, then say "Let's get into it."
3. Give a short "why this matters" block that ends in a count: three things, three problems, three ways.
4. Explain each new idea with an everyday comparison (food, building, insurance, car, body), then restate it in one short sentence.
5. Walk the screen: "make sure you...", "you can see right here...", "let me show you". Say the prompt out loud.
6. Say what a run cost and what plan you pay for, with "about" or "around". Never invent numbers.
7. Add one honest limit near any money or risk claim: "These are just example numbers."
8. Use casual tags and check-ins: "right?", "okay?", "all right?", plus "super easy", "that's it".
9. Gloss every term with "basically" or "it's just" in the next breath.
10. End with a pointer to a related video and one plain line about Claude Club, if it fits. Keep it low-key.

**Do NOT:** invent his results, follower or revenue numbers, or anecdotes; call his link offers sponsors; add emojis; use stacked jargon; promise income without a caveat; or claim ideas he credits to others (an OpenAI engineer's tweet, Karpathy's LLM council).
