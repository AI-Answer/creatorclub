# The reference: how the house style edits

Studied from Nick Saraev, "A Guide to Becoming a Solopreneur w/ Claude in 2027" (youtube.com/watch?v=wNyge-SHo1o, 18:31): every frame
at 3 s intervals, full frames at the key graphics, the scene cuts, and the speech with times. The rules in `SKILL.md` come from this.
Sound was not studied.

## Layout

- **Stage, about 85% of the video.** A near-black stage, the speaker a small portrait tile on the right edge (about an eighth of the
  frame wide, rounded corners, a soft shadow), the content in the rest of the frame.
- **Face full frame, about 10%.** Only for the hook's last line, a story or opinion, a turn to the next section, and the goodbye:
  3 to 10 s each, about once every one to two minutes.
- **The layout follows the speech.** It goes to the stage when an idea starts, and back to the face when the talk turns personal.
  Nothing changes the layout in the middle of a sentence.

## Graphics

One graphic holds one idea for as long as the idea is spoken, usually 6 to 20 s, and builds a part at a time, each part on the word that
says it. A new part every 1 to 3 s; a new graphic every 8 to 15 s.

| What is said | What is on screen | Example (spoken → shown) |
|---|---|---|
| A sentence with a point that lands | Headline, line by line, the landing line in bold sans, hairlines under each line | "The economy is really spiky and right now, especially small businesses, lack a lot of the power that tools like Claude Code allow them to get" → "The economy / **is spiky.** / Small businesses / lack what Claude Code gives." (5:46) |
| A short set, named up front | The whole set on screen dim, each part lighting as it is said, a small label above | "So three skills… build… sales… consistency" → "Three skills / Build. Sell. Stay." (1:14) |
| Two things set against each other | Versus table: two names, an italic "vs", then a row at a time, a small grey label between the sides | "One is marketing, which is generating interest. The second is sales, generation of intent" → "Marketing vs Sales / Attention · You get · A yes / A maybe · It ends in · A payment / Content · It lives in · Calls" (7:24) |
| Tools or products named one after another | A row of white tiles with each logo in one colour, the name in grey under it, each popping in as it is named | "Go High Level, very popular, HubSpot… Calendly or Cal.com" → "What the job posts ask for" + one tile per tool (6:28) |
| A chain where one thing leads to the next | Outlined boxes with thin arrows, the last a pill, in a soft panel with a serif title | "You build a growth system… the business makes more… they pay you based off the value" → "Why growth work pays: You build a growth system → The business makes more → They pay you from that value" (7:45) |
| A number worth doing the sum on | The sum written out in the serif, a small note under each term | "400 million with 146 employees… about $2.7 million per employee" → "$400M / 146 ≈ $2.7M per employee" (0:03) |
| A claim about a company or a release | The real page in a browser frame, the sentence underlined as it is said | "Lovable makes 400 million with 146 employees" → TechCrunch, "Also notable is the fact that Lovable achieved $400 million ARR with only 146…" underlined (0:00) |
| A process the creator did on screen | A screen recording or a clean rebuild of the tool's screen | Claude chats, a terminal, a spreadsheet, a phone (throughout) |

## The words on screen

- The speaker's key words, condensed: two to six words a line, never a new idea, never a summary they did not say.
- Names spelled right even where the speech-to-text heard them wrong ("Cloud Code" is Claude Code).
- Sentence case, a full stop at the end of a line that lands.
- One small label at most, grey, above the content ("Three skills", "Why this works").

## The look

- Monochrome: off-white text, grey for labels and what is not said yet, no accent colour. Emphasis is brightness and the bold sans line.
- Headlines and big words in a condensed serif; labels and box text in a plain sans.
- Hairlines, not boxes. Panels only behind charts and flows, barely lighter than the stage.
- Motion is quiet: things rise or fade in over half a second; nothing bounces, spins or flies across.

## Never

- Two graphics at once, or a graphic over the full-frame face.
- A graphic that is up before its words are said, or one that stays after the talk has moved on.
- Decoration: no stickers, emoji, arrows pointing at nothing, or icons that say nothing the words do not.
- Burned-in captions on the stage: the words on screen are the graphics.
